What I found

01

The first screen introduces the shop before the work

Your homepage opens on a rotating slideshow, led by a portrait of Joey Hamilton and the Revolt logo artwork, cycling through brand slides. The page first shows a black screen with a single loading dot, and no button sits anywhere in that first screen. A visitor deciding whether to trust this shop with a permanent piece of art meets the brand first and the proof second. Fix: open on finished work and work in progress from your own artists, with one button under it: Book a consultation.

02

Your own videos never reach your own site

Your artists post real tattoo work on Instagram: a custom spider piece from stencil to finished ink, an eagle chest piece, sleeves in progress. None of it plays on revolttattoos.com. The only video on the homepage is a Vegas Golden Knights episode, paused on its thumbnail, from another channel. Fix: put your own process reels where the slideshow sits now, playing on their own the moment the page loads.

03

Your Instagram section shows raw code instead of photos

Above the footer, where your latest work should show, the page prints the line [instagram-feed feed=1] as plain text. The photo feed behind it never loads, and every visitor who scrolls that far sees code where social proof belongs. Fix: repair the feed so the grid of recent work renders, or remove the section until it does.

04

One of your four booking buttons opens no form

The booking page offers four locations. Three buttons land on a form. The fourth, BAYBROOK, changes the web address to #baybrook and stops: no form for that shop exists on the page. The forms for the other three locations sit further down the same page, so a visitor can still book, just not at the shop they picked. Fix: publish the Baybrook form on the page, or take the button down until the form exists.

Watch the fourth button

The tap lands on BAYBROOK. The address gains #baybrook, and nothing else changes: there is no Baybrook form on this page. Scroll down, and the Las Vegas form is right there. The page still offers a booking, just not for the location the visitor chose.

05

Booking starts with a location pick, then a long form

From the homepage, booking takes a click to CONSULTATIONS, a second choice of location, then a form with 8 required items: name, email, phone, artist, a detailed description of the idea, size and placement, dates, and an age certification, all before a visitor can ask one quick question. Your phone number and email sit in the footer only. Neither appears on the first screen. Fix: one Book a consultation button in the first screen, the flagship number 702.912.4855 beside it, and the long form after a human has the conversation started.

06

Buyers who search for what you sell land on your competitors

Your site has no page for the services buyers search for by name. The sitemap lists locations, artists and booking forms, and no page for custom tattoo work. The search results in the next section show what that costs in visibility: for the searches a buyer types before knowing any shop's name, Revolt Tattoos did not appear at all. Fix: one page per service, written for that search, starting with custom tattoos.

What's working

The fame is real, and it is earned. Joey Hamilton and Walter 'Sausage' Frank founded Revolt Tattoos in 2014. Joey Hamilton won Ink Master, roundups of the best Las Vegas tattoo artists name the shop and its founder, and a search for the shop's own name puts revolttattoos.com first. Search and answer bots can read the site freely, the pages passed Google's field speed checks when measured on November 7, 2026, and the flagship carries 407 reviews on Yelp.

Unmeasured

Four things stay unmeasured in this audit: Google rankings, reply speed, what the email list sends after a visitor signs up, and this page on a phone. Each one needs a test the shop can authorize or a real device, and each is listed with its test on this page.

What I checked on your site

  • Found
    Search and answer bots can get in

    robots.txt blocks only upload folders, shopping cart links and the admin area. The home page is open, and today OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot and Claude-SearchBot each loaded it with the same 200 a browser gets.

  • Gap
    No dated quick answer

    The homepage opens with the story of founders Joey Hamilton and Walter 'Sausage' Frank and carries no date near the top. No page states the quick facts a buyer wants first, where the shop is, what work starts at, and how to book, with a date on them.

  • Gap
    Services have no pages of their own

    Locations and artists each have pages. Custom tattoos, the service buyers search for by name, has no page of its own, so there is no page for a search to return.

  • Gap
    Business data stops at a phone number

    The structured data on the homepage carries the flagship phone number, and nothing else a search engine needs: no address, no opening hours, no local business listing, and no entry for tattooing itself.

These are plain checks anyone can repeat on November 11, 2026: what the site publishes, not a score.

What I'd fix first

Fix the booking path first: one Book a consultation button and the flagship number, 702.912.4855, in the first screen (finding 5), and a form for the BAYBROOK button (finding 4). Both are small edits to pages that already exist, at the exact moment a visitor decides. The Instagram feed repair and the custom tattoos page come right behind them.

Do it yourself

The fixes, in the order to do them

1. Put booking on the first screen

Surface: Homepage, the slideshow at the top.

Do this: Add one button under the slideshow, labelled Book a consultation linked to the booking page, and beside it the flagship phone number as a call link: 702.912.4855. (Finding 5.)

Test it: Open the homepage in a private window. The button and the number are visible without scrolling.

2. Give the BAYBROOK button its form

Surface: The booking page, revolttattoos.com/artist-booking-form/.

Do this: Publish the Baybrook booking form on the page, the same form the other three locations use. Until it exists, take the BAYBROOK button off the page. (Finding 4.)

Test it: Click BAYBROOK. A booking form for that shop appears.

3. Repair the Instagram section

Surface: Homepage, the section directly above the footer.

Do this: Reconnect the feed to @revolttattoos so the grid of recent work renders in place of the line [instagram-feed feed=1]. If the plugin stays broken, replace the section with 6 stills from the shop's own posts, each linked to the shop Instagram.

Test it: Open the homepage in a private window and scroll to the footer. Photos render, not code.

4. Open on the work

Surface: Homepage slideshow, first slide.

Do this: Lead with finished tattoos and work in progress from your own artists. Joey Hamilton's portrait works as a later slide, where a visitor meets the owner after the work has spoken.

Test it: Load the homepage in a private window. The first screen shows tattoo work.

5. Give custom tattoos their own page

Surface: A new page for custom tattoos.

Do this: The page opens with the quick answer, dated: Custom tattoos at Revolt Tattoos, Fashion Show Mall, 3200 S Las Vegas Blvd STE 2680, Las Vegas. Custom work by appointment: (702) 912-4855. Updated November 2026. Write every other service page the same way: what, where, how to book, with the date on it.

Test it: Open the page. The first screen answers what, where, and how to book, with the date on it.

6. Complete the business data search engines read

Surface: The structured data (JSON-LD) on the homepage.

Do this: Add a local business entry for Revolt Tattoos carrying the name, the telephone +1-702-912-4855 that is already there, the Fashion Show Mall street address, your opening hours, and a service entry for tattooing, matching what the site shows visitors.

Test it: Run the homepage through a structured data validator. The business listing shows with no errors.
